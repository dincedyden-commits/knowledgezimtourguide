Zimbabwe's Wildest Mountain Range

Chimanimani

Chimanimani is Zimbabwe's most remote and rugged hiking destination — a range of quartzite peaks straddling the border with Mozambique, where multi-day treks lead to mountain pools, caves, and views that few international travelers ever see.

Why visit

Why visit Chimanimani

Genuine wilderness trekking

Unlike most of Zimbabwe's more accessible parks, Chimanimani requires real hiking effort to reach its best features — and rewards it with a sense of having the mountains to yourself.

Dramatic quartzite peaks

The range's distinctive white-grey rock formations and jagged peaks look unlike anywhere else in the country.

Natural mountain pools

Bridal Veil Falls and several remote rock pools offer a genuinely refreshing reward after a day of trekking.

A border-straddling range

Trails run close enough to the Mozambique border that the geography itself tells a story of two countries sharing one mountain system.

On the ground

Best things to do in Chimanimani

1

Multi-day trek to the mountain huts

Basic mountain huts within the range allow overnight stays for travelers tackling longer routes — a genuinely different style of trip from lodge-based safaris.

2

Visit Bridal Veil Falls

A more accessible waterfall near the village, reachable on a shorter half-day walk for those not doing the full mountain trek.

3

Day hike to Skeleton Pass or Peter's House

Shorter routes that still deliver the range's dramatic scenery without committing to an overnight trek.

4

Village and local culture visit

Chimanimani village itself is small, friendly, and a good base for understanding the rhythms of life in the Eastern Highlands.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

April to September offers the most stable hiking weather, with clear skies and manageable temperatures at altitude. The wet season (November to March) makes river crossings on the trails more difficult and increases the risk of sudden mist that can disorient hikers on exposed sections — guided treks become especially worthwhile during this period.

How many days you need

A two-day visit allows a day hike to Bridal Veil Falls or a shorter route, with time to enjoy the village. Three to four days suits travelers wanting a genuine overnight trek into the mountains with a stay at one of the basic huts.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Chimanimani

Village lodges and guesthouses

Small, locally-run properties in Chimanimani village serve as a comfortable base for day hikes.

Mountain huts (multi-day trekkers)

Basic, unserviced huts inside the range for those doing overnight routes — bring your own sleeping gear and food.

Self-catering cottages

A handful of cottages near the park entrance suit travelers wanting more independence.

How to get there

Chimanimani is roughly a 7-8 hour drive from Harare, or about 2 hours from Mutare, the closest sizeable town. Most travelers combine it with Nyanga and the wider Eastern Highlands as a multi-day road route rather than visiting it in isolation, given the distance involved.

Packing checklist

  • Proper trekking boots — trails are rocky and uneven
  • A backpack suited to multi-day carrying if attempting an overnight trek
  • Warm sleeping gear if staying in the mountain huts, which are unheated
  • A reliable rain shell — mist and sudden showers are common at altitude
  • More food and water than you think you'll need for multi-day routes
