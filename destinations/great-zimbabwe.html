A 1,000-Year-Old Stone City

Great Zimbabwe

Great Zimbabwe is the largest pre-colonial stone structure in sub-Saharan Africa — the ruins of a city that was home to an estimated 18,000 people at its peak in the 14th century, and the very namesake of the country.

Why visit

Why visit Great Zimbabwe

The country's namesake monument

"Zimbabwe" is believed to derive from the Shona phrase for "stone houses" — and this site, built without mortar from carefully shaped granite blocks, is the reason.

A UNESCO World Heritage Site

Recognised since 1986 for its outstanding architectural and historical significance as the centre of a major trading civilisation.

Architecture you won't see described accurately in passing

The Great Enclosure's curved granite walls, built without any binding mortar, are still structurally intact after roughly 700 years.

A quieter, more contemplative stop

Compared to Victoria Falls or Hwange, Great Zimbabwe sees far fewer visitors, which makes for an unhurried, personal experience of the site.

On the ground

Best things to do in Great Zimbabwe

1

Guided walk through the Great Enclosure

The site's most iconic structure — a curved, free-standing stone wall up to 11 metres high. A local guide's context on how it was likely used changes the visit completely.

2

Climb to the Hill Complex

The original royal residence, built into granite boulders on a hill overlooking the valley — a moderate climb rewarded by sweeping views and the oldest part of the site.

3

Visit the on-site museum

Houses artefacts including the famous Zimbabwe Birds, soapstone carvings that are now a national symbol, found throughout the ruins.

4

Explore the Valley Enclosures

Lower-lying residential ruins that give a sense of how the wider city was laid out beyond the royal and ceremonial structures.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

Great Zimbabwe is enjoyable any time of year since most of the visit is outdoors among the ruins. The dry season (April to October) offers the most comfortable walking conditions and clearer long-distance views from the Hill Complex. The rainy season brings dramatic green scenery to the surrounding valley but can make some of the granite walking surfaces slippery.

How many days you need

A full day is enough to properly explore the Hill Complex, Great Enclosure, Valley Enclosures, and museum with a guide. Many travelers combine it with an overnight stay nearby to avoid a rushed same-day round trip from Harare or Masvingo.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Great Zimbabwe

Lodges near the ruins

A small number of lodges sit within walking or short driving distance of the site entrance, the most convenient option for an early start.

Masvingo town hotels

About 30 minutes from the ruins, with more dining options if you want a town base.

Lake Mutirikwi properties

A nearby lake offers a scenic, relaxed overnight option if you want to extend your stay in the area.

How to get there

Great Zimbabwe sits about 30km from Masvingo town, roughly a 4-5 hour drive south of Harare, or around 5-6 hours from Victoria Falls/Bulawayo — a route often combined with a wider Zimbabwe road trip rather than visited in isolation. Domestic flights connect Harare to Masvingo for travelers short on time.

Packing checklist

  • Comfortable closed walking shoes — the Hill Complex involves uneven granite steps
  • Sun protection — much of the site has limited shade
  • Water for the walk between enclosures
  • A camera; photography is permitted throughout the site
