Zimbabwe's Capital City

Harare

Harare is Zimbabwe's capital and the practical starting point for many international itineraries — a city with a genuine art scene, jacaranda-lined streets in spring, and a more grounded, everyday view of Zimbabwean life than the country's safari circuit alone provides.

Why visit

Why visit Harare

A genuine entry point for most international flights

Harare's airport is the main hub connecting Zimbabwe to international and regional routes, making it a natural first or last stop.

One of Africa's strongest sculpture traditions

Zimbabwean stone sculpture, particularly Shona sculpture, has an internationally recognised reputation, and Harare's galleries are the best place to see and buy it directly from artists.

Jacaranda season

Each October, the city's streets turn purple as jacaranda trees bloom — a striking, photogenic season worth timing a stopover around if it fits your dates.

A break from the safari circuit

Markets, restaurants, and city life offer genuine contrast if your itinerary is otherwise built around parks and waterfalls.

On the ground

Best things to do in Harare

1

National Gallery of Zimbabwe

The country's leading gallery for Shona stone sculpture and contemporary Zimbabwean art, with rotating exhibitions worth checking ahead of a visit.

2

Mbare Musika market

A large, genuinely local market for produce, crafts, and everyday city life — best visited with a guide for context and comfort.

3

Chapungu Sculpture Park

An open-air park showcasing large-scale stone sculpture in a garden setting, more relaxed than a formal gallery visit.

4

Borrowdale and avenues exploration

A more residential, leafy side of the city worth a slow drive through during jacaranda season.

5

Day trip to Lake Chivero

A short drive from the city centre for birdwatching and a change of pace from urban streets.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

April through September offers the most comfortable weather for walking the city, with mild days and minimal rain. If the jacaranda bloom matters to you, October is the specific window to target, even though it sits at the edge of the rainy season's onset.

How many days you need

One full day covers the National Gallery, a market visit, and dinner at one of the city's better restaurants. Two days allows a more relaxed pace, including Chapungu Sculpture Park or a day trip to Lake Chivero.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Harare

City centre hotels

Closest to the National Gallery, restaurants, and business district — best for short stopovers.

Borrowdale and northern suburbs

Quieter, leafier areas with some of the city's best restaurants, suited to a slightly longer stay.

Airport-area hotels

Practical for very short layovers or early-morning onward flights.

How to get there

Robert Gabriel Mugabe International Airport (HRE) connects Harare to regional African hubs and several international routes, and sits about 30 minutes from the city centre. From Harare, road and air connections reach Victoria Falls, Hwange, Nyanga, Great Zimbabwe, and most other major destinations covered on this site.

Packing checklist

  • Smart-casual clothing if planning restaurant evenings or gallery visits
  • A light jacket for cooler evenings, especially June to August
  • Comfortable shoes for walking markets and galleries
  • Cash in small denominations for market purchases
