Zimbabwe's Largest National Park

Hwange National Park

Hwange is Zimbabwe's largest national park and home to one of Africa's biggest elephant populations — sometimes 500-strong herds gathering at waterholes in the dry season. It's a serious safari destination without the crowds of more famous East African parks.

Why visit

Why visit Hwange National Park

One of Africa's great elephant strongholds

Hwange holds one of the continent's largest elephant populations, with dry-season waterhole gatherings that can number in the hundreds.

Genuine wilderness, fewer vehicles

At over 14,600 km², Hwange is bigger than Connecticut, but receives a fraction of the visitor numbers of Etosha or the Serengeti — sightings rarely come with a dozen other vehicles.

All of the Big Five, plus painted wolves

Lion, leopard, buffalo, and rhino (in managed areas) are all present, alongside one of Zimbabwe's most viable populations of endangered African painted wolves (wild dogs).

Flexible safari styles

Game drives, walking safaris, and hide-based waterhole photography are all possible here, often within the same trip.

On the ground

Best things to do in Hwange National Park

1

Dawn and dusk game drives

The two windows when wildlife is most active — early morning and the hours before dusk — are when most sightings happen. A good guide reads tracks and listens for alarm calls along the way.

2

Waterhole hide photography

Several pumped waterholes have permanent hides where you can sit quietly for hours and let elephant herds, kudu, and predators come to you — excellent for photographers.

3

Guided walking safari

Shorter walks led by an armed, licensed guide focus on tracking, plant life, and the smaller details a vehicle drives past.

4

Night drives (select areas)

Some private concessions bordering the park allow night drives, when nocturnal species like hyena, civet, and sometimes leopard become active.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

The dry season, roughly July to October, is when Hwange's wildlife viewing peaks — natural water sources dry up and animals concentrate around the park's pumped waterholes, making sightings far more predictable. The rainy season (November to March) turns the bush green and lush, which is beautiful and good for birding, but animals disperse widely and game drives require more patience.

How many days you need

Three nights is the realistic minimum to get a feel for Hwange's size and variety — enough for several game drives covering different areas of the park. Four nights allows time to add a walking safari or a full day at a waterhole hide without feeling rushed.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Hwange National Park

Safari lodges inside the park

Permanent lodges within Hwange itself offer the shortest drive times to game-viewing areas and often have their own waterholes on site.

Private concession camps

Bordering properties on private land often allow night drives and off-road tracking that the national park itself restricts.

Budget self-catering camps

A smaller number of simple, fenced campsites suit travelers doing a self-drive safari on a tighter budget.

How to get there

Hwange is roughly 1.5–2.5 hours by road from Victoria Falls, depending on which part of the park you're staying in — a route we run regularly. There's also a small airstrip (Hwange National Park Airport, HWN) served by light aircraft charters from Victoria Falls, which is worth considering if you're short on time.

Packing checklist

  • Neutral-coloured clothing (khaki, olive, brown) for game drives
  • A warm layer for early morning drives — temperatures drop sharply before sunrise even in the dry season
  • Binoculars (we can also provide a pair)
  • A camera with some zoom reach, if photography matters to you
  • Insect repellent, particularly in the wetter months
