Mountains, Waterfalls & Cool Air

Nyanga

Nyanga sits in Zimbabwe's Eastern Highlands, a region of rolling mountains, pine forests, and waterfalls that feels almost nothing like the lowland safari country most visitors expect. It's the country's go-to escape for cooler air and genuinely good hiking.

Why visit

Why visit Nyanga

Zimbabwe's highest mountain

Mount Nyangani, at 2,592 metres, is the country's tallest peak and a genuinely rewarding day hike for reasonably fit travelers.

Zimbabwe's tallest waterfall

The Mtarazi Falls drop around 762 metres, one of Africa's highest single-drop waterfalls, reached via a short, scenic walk.

A completely different climate and landscape

Pine forests, trout streams, and misty mountain air offer a deliberate contrast to a safari-heavy itinerary.

Genuinely good trout fishing

Introduced over a century ago, trout fishing in Nyanga's dams and rivers remains a quiet, well-loved local pastime.

On the ground

Best things to do in Nyanga

1

Hike Mount Nyangani

A 4–6 hour round trip depending on route and fitness, with a permit and registration required at the park gate. Weather changes fast at altitude — check conditions before setting out.

2

Visit Mtarazi Falls viewpoint

A short, well-maintained trail leads to a viewing platform looking down the full height of the falls into the valley below.

3

Explore the Nyangwe and Nyahokwe ruins

Lesser-known stone terracing and ruins from an earlier farming civilisation, scattered through the highlands and far less visited than Great Zimbabwe.

4

Trout fishing on a private dam

Several lodges offer access to stocked dams for half or full-day fly fishing, equipment usually included.

5

Drive the Eastern Highlands scenic route

Connecting Nyanga to nearby Honde Valley offers dramatic changes in elevation and vegetation within a single afternoon drive.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

April to September is the most comfortable window — cool, clear mountain air without the heaviest rains. The wet season (November to March) brings mist and rain that can obscure views and make trails slippery, though it also makes the waterfalls more dramatic. Nights are cold year-round at this altitude, even when the lowlands are sweltering.

How many days you need

Two days allows time for the Mount Nyangani hike and the Mtarazi Falls viewpoint without rushing. Three days gives room to add fishing, a ruins visit, or simply more time to enjoy the cooler climate and slower pace.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Nyanga

Mountain lodges

Several established lodges sit within the national park or just outside it, often with fireplaces — genuinely useful given the cold nights.

Self-catering cabins

Popular with Zimbabwean families and a good-value option if you want a kitchen and a slower pace.

Trout-fishing focused properties

A smaller number of lodges built specifically around private dam access for anglers.

How to get there

Nyanga is roughly a 4-5 hour drive east from Harare on a well-maintained tarred road, making it a realistic add-on to a Harare-based itinerary. There's no commercial airport directly in Nyanga, so all visitors arrive by road, either self-driven or with a transfer we can arrange.

Packing checklist

  • Warm layers, including for evenings — temperatures drop well below what the rest of Zimbabwe experiences
  • Proper hiking shoes for Mount Nyangani and Mtarazi Falls trails
  • A rain shell, even in the dry season — mountain weather shifts quickly
  • Fishing gear if you have your own preferred setup (rentals are available)
