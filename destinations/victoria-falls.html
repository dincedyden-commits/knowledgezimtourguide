Why visit Victoria Falls

One of the Seven Natural Wonders

The Falls drop up to 108 metres into the Batoka Gorge â€” louder, wider, and more physically overwhelming up close than photos ever convey.

The widest sheet of falling water on Earth

At over 1.7km wide, Victoria Falls (locally, Mosi-oa-Tunya â€” "The Smoke That Thunders") holds that title outright.

More adrenaline activities than anywhere else in Zimbabwe

Bungee jumping off the bridge, white-water rafting grade 5 rapids, helicopter flights, and gorge swings are all based right in town.

A genuinely walkable town

Victoria Falls town is small enough to explore on foot, with restaurants, curio markets, and lodges all within a short walk of the Falls themselves.

On the ground

Best things to do in Victoria Falls

1

Walk the Victoria Falls rainforest trail

A series of 16 viewpoints along the Zimbabwean side let you walk the full length of the Falls. Expect to get soaked at certain viewpoints during high-water months â€” bring a dry bag for your phone.

2

Sunset cruise on the Zambezi

A relaxed two-hour boat cruise upstream from the Falls, usually with drinks included, and a good chance of spotting hippos, elephants, and crocodiles along the banks.

3

Devil's Pool or Livingstone Island (seasonal)

Only possible from the Zambian side and only when water levels are low enough (roughly Augâ€“Jan) â€” we can arrange the transfer if your dates line up.

4

White-water rafting on the Zambezi

Grade 3â€“5 rapids below the Falls, running roughly February to early January depending on water levels. A full-day, genuinely physical activity â€” not for first-time swimmers.

5

Helicopter flight over the Falls

The only way to see the full scale of the gorge system and the Zambezi's zigzag course below the Falls. 13 or 25-minute flights are both worth it.

6

Victoria Falls Bridge walk or bungee

Built in 1905, the bridge connects Zimbabwe and Zambia and is the starting point for the 111m bungee jump, if you want it.

Timing it right

Best time to visit & how long to stay

Best time to visit

Victoria Falls is a year-round destination, but what you see changes a lot by season. From March to May, after the rainy season, the Falls run at their fullest â€” spectacular, but spray can obscure the view from some angles. By July to October, water levels drop and visibility improves dramatically, which is also when activities like Devil's Pool become possible. November to February brings the lowest water (sometimes a trickle on the Zimbabwean side in late season) but also the cheapest rates and quietest crowds.

How many days you need

Two full days covers the essentials â€” one day for the Falls themselves and a sunset cruise, one for an adrenaline activity or a half-day at leisure. If you're combining adventure activities (rafting, bungee, gorge swing) with a relaxed cruise and the rainforest walk, three days gives you breathing room without feeling rushed.

Basing yourself here

Where to stay in Victoria Falls

Riverside lodges

A handful of properties sit directly on the Zambezi a short drive from town, with elephants and hippos visible from the deck most evenings. Best for travelers prioritizing wildlife atmosphere over walkability.

In-town hotels

Walking distance to the Falls entrance, restaurants, and curio markets. Best for travelers who want to explore on foot without arranging transport for every outing.

Boutique guesthouses

Smaller, locally-run properties just outside the town centre â€” quieter, often better value, and a good fit if you want a more personal stay.

How to get there

Victoria Falls has its own international airport (VFA) with direct flights from Johannesburg, Cape Town, and several regional hubs. If you're combining Victoria Falls with Hwange or other parks, road transfers of 1.5â€“3 hours are common and something we arrange directly. Crossing into Zambia for the day is straightforward at the border post beside the bridge â€” bring your passport and KAZA UniVisa if you have one.

Packing checklist

  • A light rain jacket or poncho (spray from the Falls reaches the walking trails)
  • A dry bag or waterproof phone pouch
  • Closed shoes with grip for the rainforest trail
  • Sunscreen and a hat â€” there's little shade on the bridge and gorge viewpoints
  • A light jacket for early morning cruises, even in the dry season
